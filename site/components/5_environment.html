5. Environmental Modulation: The Discriminating Test

A defining prediction of TEP is environmental screening. Whereas MOND is driven primarily by the internal acceleration of the binary, TEP predicts that the screening radius $R_s$ should depend on the ambient gravitational environment.

5.1 Galactocentric Stratification

Under TEP, binaries embedded in more strongly screening environmental states remain suppressed to larger separations than binaries in more weakly screening environments. In the density-scaling benchmark adopted here, proximity to the Galactic midplane provides the corresponding higher-density environmental projection. Systems close to the Galactic midplane should therefore show a later transition than systems at greater vertical height, where the ambient density is lower.

This prediction can be tested by stratifying the Gaia DR3 sample by vertical height above the Galactic plane ($|Z|$). The midplane subsample ($|Z| < 100$ pc) lies well within the thin-disk scale height ($\sim 300$ pc), where baryonic density is highest. The high-$|Z|$ subsample ($|Z| > 150$ pc) samples the thick disk and disk–halo transition, where ambient stellar density is measurably lower. A $100$–$150$ pc buffer excludes systems with ambiguous classification. Using metallicity-corrected masses and parametric-resampling uncertainty propagation, the analysis yields:

  • Midplane / High Density ($|Z| < 100$ pc): $R_s = 7{,}131 \pm 1{,}341$ AU.
  • High-$|Z|$ / Low Density ($|Z| > 150$ pc): $R_s = 4{,}662 \pm 196$ AU.

Because the two subsamples have different population mixes, allowing $\alpha_{\rm sat}$ to float freely in each would introduce an amplitude–scale degeneracy that complicates the comparison of $R_s$. The saturation amplitude is therefore fixed at $\alpha_{\rm sat} = 0.4$ for both subsamples, close to the full-sample best fit ($0.366$), so that $R_s$ absorbs only the transition-scale information. Under this constraint, the high-$|Z|$ transition radius remains smaller than the midplane value. This is the central environmental signature required by TEP: in the lower-density environment, screening fails earlier.

When $R_s$ and $\alpha_{\rm sat}$ are fitted independently in each subsample, their strong covariance prevents either transition scale from being identified separately. The joint profile-likelihood model therefore estimates a common underlying asymptotic channel response while allowing the environmental recovery scales to differ. The shared amplitude is not merely a statistical convenience: under the TEP screening semantics the saturation amplitude is the environment-shared asymptotic Temporal-Shear response, bounded by the Galactic ambient floor (Section 2.1), while the environment modulates the *scale* at which that response recovers. A model that permits different plateaus would test a different hypothesis—an intrinsically environment-dependent response amplitude—than the screening transition TEP predicts. The joint fit yields $R_s^{\rm midplane} = 4{,}912 \pm 792$ AU and $R_s^{\rm high\text{-}|Z|} = 3{,}447 \pm 366$ AU, with $\Delta\chi^2 = 60.3$ ($p = 8.1 \times 10^{-15}$, 1 degree of freedom). The ordering is preserved in 100\% of parametric-resampling realizations. Because $\alpha_{\rm sat}$ is profiled from the data rather than imposed externally, the joint fit eliminates the arbitrary fixed-amplitude choice. Figure 5.3 further shows that the apparent free-fit inversion lies along the $R_s$–$\alpha_{\rm sat}$ degeneracy valley, whereas the global joint solution robustly preserves the TEP environmental ordering. The environmental inference is consequently based on the joint likelihood surface, not on the unstable marginal minima of two separately underconstrained fits.

This conclusion does not rest on the metallicity correction alone. When the analysis is restricted to the solar-track subsample—where an empirical mass-luminosity calibration can be used without any color-dependent correction—the same ordering is recovered: $R_s = 4{,}145 \pm 276$ AU in the high-$|Z|$ subsample and $R_s = 6{,}856 \pm 920$ AU in the midplane. Permutation tests yield $p < 10^{-4}$ for the full-sample ordering and $p < 10^{-3}$ for the solar-track control. That replication under an independent calibration makes the environmental signal much harder to dismiss as a mass-model artifact. The solar-track selection also answers a subtler demographic concern: because $|Z|$ correlates with age and metallicity in the Galaxy, the full-sample $|Z|$ subsamples could differ in their inner-binary demographics. Selecting only stars on the zero-correction solar-metallicity sequence holds the metallicity axis of that correlation fixed by construction, so the surviving ordering at $p < 10^{-3}$ cannot be attributed to a metallicity-correlated inner-binary population. The residual uncovariate is the age-correlated inner-binary channel, addressed explicitly in Section 6.

Additional stratified controls confirm that the signal is not generated by a single distance shell, stellar subpopulation, or analysis choice. Splitting at the median distance yields a finite-transition preference in both halves ($R_s = 3{,}551 \pm 420$ AU near; $R_s = 10{,}307 \pm 7{,}050$ AU far), although the distant subset is much less precise. Parallel splits by mass ratio and primary mass also preserve the transition morphology across all four half-samples. A leave-one-bin-out test drops each of the 19 separation bins in turn: the environmental ordering is preserved in all 19/19 cases, confirming that no single bin drives the result. Matched-subsample controls further isolate the signal from demographic confounders: restricting both populations to overlapping distance support, overlapping color support, or a stricter astrometric quality cut (RUWE $< 1.1$) all preserve the ordering. Alternative binning schemes ($12$ and $18$ bins in place of the fiducial 19) likewise leave the ordering intact. Across all 5 matched controls, the ordering $R_s({\rm midplane}) > R_s({\rm high\text{-}}|Z|)$ is preserved in every case.

A mass-resolved measurement supplies an orthogonal check on the transition's physicality. Stratifying the full RUWE-selected sample into mass terciles (median masses $0.74$, $1.20$, and $1.70\,M_\odot$) and applying the same shared-$\alpha_{\rm sat}$ joint fit yields transition scales that rise monotonically with system mass: $R_s = 2{,}778$, $2{,}966$, and $4{,}848$ AU ($\alpha_{\rm sat} = 0.327 \pm 0.062$ shared). A log-space power-law fit gives the mass-scaling exponent $n = 0.64$ in $R_s \propto M^n$; bootstrap resampling returns a median exponent of $0.64$, with 86% of resamples positive and 66% strictly monotone. A transition localized by a gravity-like field scale must grow with system mass; a mass-invariant artifact (detector, calibration, or population effect) would not. The measured exponent is consistent with the derived radius law $R_s = \sqrt{GM/g_t} \propto M^{1/2}$ of the canonical operator (Smawfield 2025a), matching the $M^{1/2}$ scaling expected under MOND as well. Therefore, while mass scaling verifies the field nature of the transition, the primary discriminators between TEP and MOND remain the transition shape (flux-conserving recovery versus a deep-MOND tail) and the environmental ordering.

A complementary covariate-hardened test removes the profile-fit layer entirely and asks directly whether residual $\tilde v$ varies with environment once the known observational confounds are held fixed. The raw pool-level correlation between $\tilde v$ and $|Z|$ is distance-confounded—high-$|Z|$ pairs are systematically more distant and carry a higher proper-motion noise floor—so separation, distance, noise-floor, and mass are fixed in fine decile cells and the residual $\tilde v$ is regressed on $|Z|$ within each separation window with heteroscedasticity-consistent errors. The environmental coupling is absent at tight separations ($t = +0.7$ at $s < 0.5$ kAU), rises steadily through the transition window ($t = +2.2$, $+2.8$, $+3.7$ at $s = 0.5$–$1.5$, $1.5$–$3$, $3$–$5$ kAU), and reaches a slope of $+0.025$ per $100$ pc at $s = 5$–$9$ kAU with $t = +12.7$ ($+11.9$ after adding a metallicity covariate). The coupling is monotone in $|Z|$ within every separation bin, and a direct high- versus low-$|Z|$ contrast at $s > 3$ kAU gives $+0.043 \pm 0.006$ ($6.9\sigma$, positive in 100% of resamples). The separation localization is the discriminating morphology: a uniform distance- or noise-related artifact cannot concentrate the $|Z|$ response specifically in the wide-separation window where the Temporal-Shear response is predicted to act. The interpretation is bounded by two candidate astrophysical alternatives—thick-disk orbital-population differences and Galactic-tide survival—which are quantitatively constrained and partially closed by the controls below rather than left fully open (Section 6).

A further control frees the eccentricity prior itself. The residual profile structure previously attributed to response-family mismatch proves to be dominated by the assumed eccentricity law: when the eccentricity distribution is fitted as a free mixture of thermal, superthermal, and uniform components jointly with the ambient response parameter $u_{\rm eff}$ on the selection-safe window (common random numbers and the measured per-pair proper-motion-error noise kernel), every $|Z|$ stratum selects the uniform-dominated component ($w_{\rm uniform} \geq 0.8$), the absolute profiles fit at $\chi^2/{\rm dof} \approx 1$–$8$, and the ambient ordering remains monotone ($u_{\rm eff} = 0.40 \to 0.15$ from midplane to high-$|Z|$). Under the data-preferred mixture the density-scaling exponent is $p \approx 0.7$, giving an honest exponent band $p \approx 0.5$–$0.7$ across eccentricity-law systematics, centered on the $\rho^{1/2}$ benchmark but not precision-pinned. The noise-aware Newtonian null remains rejected at $\Delta\chi^2 \approx 800$–$1{,}900$ per stratum under the same best-fit mixture. Two features constrain the alternatives above: the fitted eccentricity weights are $|Z|$-independent, so a thick-disk orbital-population explanation would require an e-law composition that varies with height yet produces identical mixture weights—a requirement the data do not support—and the Newtonian null is evaluated under the identical mixture and noise kernel, so its rejection cannot be attributed to the prior choice.

Galactic-tide survival is quantitatively bounded at the relevant separations. At solar-circle enclosed mass ($M_{\rm enc} \approx 10^{11}\,M_\odot$) the Jacobi radius of a typical pair is $r_J \approx 250$–$280$ kAU; pairs in the response window $s = 5$–$9$ kAU sit at a median $s/r_J \approx 0.023$, and even in the widest sample bin ($s = 9$–$30$ kAU) only 3.5% exceed $s/r_J = 0.1$. Beyond $\sim 30$ kAU the tide does become the dominant control (median $s/r_J \approx 0.13$), which bounds the reach of any pair-intrinsic inference rather than challenging the response-window signal. Differential tidal truncation between midplane and high-$|Z|$ pairs therefore cannot manufacture an effect localized fortyfold inside the tidal radius. The remaining tide-related channel—preferential survival of diffuse high-$|Z|$ pairs through earlier evolutionary truncation—affects pair demographics rather than the measured $\tilde v$ ordering within surviving bound pairs.

The widest-separation behaviour, where the hard purity cut truncates the $\tilde v$ tail, is recovered by replacing the cut with bound-probability weighting ($w = 1 - R_{\rm chance}$): under soft weighting the normalized profiles no longer decline but rise monotonically to the last bin in every stratum, and the absolute high- versus low-$|Z|$ median contrast grows from $+0.040 \pm 0.005$ ($8.4\sigma$) at $s = 0.5$–$2$ kAU to $+0.094 \pm 0.018$ ($5.3\sigma$) at $s = 15$–$30$ kAU. A CDF-level mixture deconvolution then isolates the bound component explicitly, using the clearly unbound population ($R_{\rm chance} > 0.2$) as the interloper shape. Because $R_{\rm chance}$ is a prior that can underestimate the true interloper fraction at wide $s$—the $\tilde v > 1.5$ tail allows up to $\sim$30% contamination in the widest decade—the deconvolution is evaluated under interloper fractions of $0.11$ (the $R_{\rm chance}$ estimate), $0.20$, and $0.30$. The bound-component median at $s = 20$–$50$ kAU remains $0.86$–$1.11$ across strata, elevated above the $s < 2$ kAU baseline of $0.64$–$0.69$ under every fraction tested. The outer-bin downturn in the hard-cut profiles is therefore an artifact of the purity threshold itself, not a property of the bound population; the transition radii quoted earlier from full-range fits are correspondingly biased low for the unsaturated dilute strata, and the selection-safe statements are the monotone ordering and the bound-component elevation reported here.

5.2 Chameleon-Completion Benchmark

TEP itself does not commit to a specific microscopic screening mechanism (Smawfield 2025a, §A4, §7), so the TEP-native macroscopic prediction for environmental modulation is that stronger environmental/source screening delays Temporal-Shear recovery. To make this quantitative the screening radius is parameterized as an empirical power law in ambient baryonic density, $R_s \propto \rho_{\rm amb}^{p}$ (the inverse-power chameleon form that motivates this parameterization—$V(\phi) \propto \phi^{-n}$ giving $p = 1/(n+1)$ under thin-shell matching, Ratra & Peebles 1988; Khoury & Weltman 2004—is excluded under the TEP coupling convention $\beta_A = -1$; Smawfield 2025a, F1). In the density-scaling benchmark this becomes the specific relation denser environment → larger $R_s$. The specific power-law exponent is an empirical property of the environmental response, not of a specified microscopic completion. The ratio of transition radii at two Galactic heights then depends only on the ambient density ratio and the scaling exponent:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:environment_ratio} \frac{R_s(Z_2)}{R_s(Z_1)} = \left(\frac{\rho_{\rm amb}(Z_2)}{\rho_{\rm amb}(Z_1)}\right)^{p} \end{equation}

Using a standard three-component Galactic baryonic density model—stellar thin disk ($\rho_0 = 0.040\;M_\odot\,{\rm pc}^{-3}$, $h = 300$ pc), thick disk ($0.005\;M_\odot\,{\rm pc}^{-3}$, $h = 900$ pc), and gas disk ($0.050\;M_\odot\,{\rm pc}^{-3}$, $h = 150$ pc; McKee, Parravano & Hollenbach 2015; Bovy 2015)—the median heights of the two subsamples ($|Z| = 47$ pc and $248$ pc) correspond to densities $\rho = 0.075$ and $0.031\;M_\odot\,{\rm pc}^{-3}$ respectively, giving a density ratio of $0.41$. The benchmark exponent $p = 1/2$ then predicts $R_s({\rm high}\text{-}|Z|)/R_s({\rm midplane}) = 0.64$, compared to the observed joint-fit ratio of $0.702 \pm 0.074$. Calibrating with the midplane joint-fit value ($R_s = 4{,}912$ AU), the $p = 1/2$ scaling predicts $R_s({\rm high}\text{-}|Z|) = 3{,}144$ AU, consistent with the observed $3{,}447 \pm 355$ AU.

Inverting the scaling relation to infer $p$ from the data yields $p = 0.40 \pm 0.14$ (parametric resampling), consistent with the benchmark value $p = 1/2$ within uncertainty. The large uncertainty reflects the modest lever arm between the two height bins; a finer five-bin $|Z|$ stratification (Section 6.4, Ninth) gives $p = 0.50 \pm 0.03$ under the anchored one-parameter estimator, but a free two-parameter fit to the same five radii returns $p = 0.36\text{--}0.39$ with a rescaled uncertainty of order $\pm 0.1$, so the honest data-side statement is $p \approx 0.4 \pm 0.1$ — consistent with both the $\rho^{1/2}$ benchmark and the $\rho^{1/3}$ ambient channel that the noise-corrected inversion independently returns ($0.40 \pm 0.05$). The key point for TEP is not that any particular completion is required, but that within this tractable benchmark the observed $R_s$ ratio follows a simple density scaling without ad hoc tuning. Given a Galactic baryonic density model and one calibration point, the density-scaling benchmark renders $\epsilon_{\rm env}$ semi-predictive at arbitrary heights, and that prediction is consistent with the observed high-$|Z|$ transition radius.
