The Gaia DR3 catalog of over one million wide binaries opens a precise window onto gravity in the weak-field regime (a ≲ 10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻²), yet whether the observed velocity excess reflects modified gravity or unresolved systematics remains contested.

In the Temporal Equivalence Principle (TEP), a conformal scalar field modulates matter proper time as

dτ/dt ≈ A(φ), A(φ)=exp(β_Aφ/M_Pl).

The Cepheid-calibrated response scale is denoted κ_Cep, while the wide-binary transition is parameterized independently by the velocity-profile saturation amplitude αₛₐₜ, not by a bare scalar coupling. This paper tests whether the Gaia wide-binary anomaly is better described as smooth Temporal Shear recovery in weak-field environments.

From 341,315 high-purity systems, the analysis identifies a screening transition at Rₛ = 2,646 ± 182 AU (statistical; ± 609 AU total), strongly preferred over both a flat Newtonian profile (Δ χ² = 14,845) and a constant boost (Δ χ² = 3,583). The transition is strongly preferred under diagonal, AR(1) and Gaussian-process covariance treatments; even the most conservative covariance-aware comparison gives Δχ² ≥ 1,073 against a flat Newtonian profile and Δχ² ≥ 284 against a constant boost. At large separation the profile saturates at αₛₐₜ = 0.366 ± 0.012, roughly 35–40% above the Keplerian baseline. Broader smooth-transition fits preserve the same few-thousand-AU onset.

The signal also shows the environmental ordering required by TEP. With a non-circular metallicity guardrail that uses a conservative external β_MLR prior unless independent spectroscopic metallicities are cached, the lower-density high-|Z| population transitions at smaller radius than the higher-density midplane. The headline comparison is the joint profile-likelihood fit, which shares one saturation amplitude across environments—the physically prescribed constraint, since screening modulates the recovery scale while the ambient-floor-limited plateau is a shared response at fixed environment—and returns Rₛ = 3,447 ± 366 versus 4,912 ± 792 AU with Δχ² = 60.3 (p = 8.1 × 10⁻¹⁵), the ordering preserved in 100% of resamples. The model-free permutation test confirms the ordering at p ≤ 10⁻⁴ (0 exceedances in 10,000 realizations; fixed-amplitude protocol: Rₛ = 4,662 ± 196 versus 7,131 ± 1,341 AU), as does the solar-track control (Rₛ = 4,145 ± 276 versus 6,856 ± 920 AU; p ≤ 10⁻³), whose zero-metallicity-correction selection additionally breaks the |Z|–metallicity correlation by construction. The transition scale furthermore rises with system mass (Rₛ = 2,778, 2,966, 4,848 AU across mass terciles; measured exponent n = 0.64 with 86% of resamples positive), consistent with the derived radius law Rₛ ∝ M¹/2. A five-bin |Z| stratification confirms the density-dependent scaling: the environmental ordering is quantified by an empirical density-scaling exponent, Rₛ ∝ ρ_amb^p with p ≈ 0.4 ± 0.1 under free fits of the same stratified radii—consistent with the square-root benchmark p = 1/2 and the ρ¹/3 ambient channel alike (TEP does not commit to a specific microscopic mechanism; the inverse-power chameleon form that motivated the parameterization is excluded for the TEP coupling β_A = -1, Smawfield 2025a, F1). The ordering itself is selection-independent: restricted to the window below 9 kAU where the purity cut removes fewer than 2% of pairs, the per-separation median-ṽordering is monotone in |Z| in every bin, reaches +0.043 ± 0.006 (6.9σ, 100% bootstrap-positive) at s > 3 kAU, and is unchanged with no purity cut applied at all. Under bound-probability weighting the profiles rise monotonically to the widest measured separations, the absolute high- versus low-|Z| contrast grows to +0.094 ± 0.018 (5.3σ) at s = 15–30 kAU, and a mixture deconvolution leaves the bound-component median elevated at s = 20–50 kAU under interloper fractions up to three times the prior estimate. Scrambling tests and phase-mixed Newtonian orbital forward models fail to reproduce the observed screening preference. The wide-binary anomaly is therefore not a generic low-acceleration excess but a structured, environmentally modulated screening transition—one whose morphology, onset scale, and environmental ordering are quantitatively consistent with the conformal scalar field of TEP and are not reproduced by the Newtonian orbital-projection or MOND/EFE parameterizations tested here.
