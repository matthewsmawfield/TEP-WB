2. Theoretical Framework: Temporal Shear Recovery

Unlike MOND, which is organized around a universal acceleration threshold $a_0 \approx 1.2 \times 10^{-10}$ m/s$^2$ (Milgrom 1983), the Temporal Equivalence Principle (TEP) is organized around Temporal Shear recovery in weak-field environments of a conformal scalar field. In the full TEP framework (Smawfield 2025a), matter couples to a physical metric $\tilde{g}_{\mu \nu} = A^2(\phi)\,g_{\mu \nu} + B(\phi)\,\nabla_\mu\phi\,\nabla_\nu\phi$, where $A(\phi) = \exp(\beta_A\phi/M_{\text{Pl}})$ is the universal conformal factor and $B(\phi)$ encodes small disformal deformations (Smawfield 2025a). Wide binaries probe separations and velocities far from the relativistic limit, so the disformal sector is negligible and only the conformal sector matters here. In that limit matter proper time is modulated as $\mathrm{d}\tau/\mathrm{d}t \approx A(\phi)$, with $\alpha(\phi) \equiv \mathrm{d}\ln A/\mathrm{d}\phi$ the conformal coupling strength. The underlying screening transition is associated with a Temporal Topology saturation scale $\rho_T \approx 20$ g/cm$^3$ (Smawfield 2025g), which is not a binary on/off threshold but a non-linear saturation scale of the conformal-factor sector. In the language of scalar-tensor theory, the gravitational metric $g_{\mu\nu}$ corresponds to the Einstein frame and the matter metric $\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}$ to the Jordan frame; the kinematic observables measured here—orbital velocities and projected separations—are defined in the Jordan frame, where matter propagates and clocks tick. The conformal factor $A(\phi)$ therefore enters the velocity ratio $\tilde{v}$ directly through its effect on matter-frame proper time, not merely as a post-Newtonian correction to the Einstein-frame potential. This frame distinction is what ties the kinematic enhancement to the clock-rate signature in Section 2.3.

In the weak-field limit, writing $\Theta = \ln A$, the matter-frame potential is $\Phi_{\rm eff} \simeq \Phi_N + c^2\Theta$. Matter-frame motion therefore responds to

\begin{equation} \label{eq:effective_acceleration} \mathbf{a}_{\rm eff} = -\nabla\Phi_N - c^2\,\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{\rm obs}, \qquad \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{\rm obs} = \mathcal{S}_\Sigma(\mathcal{E})\,\nabla\ln A \end{equation}

where $\mathcal{S}_\Sigma(\mathcal{E})$ is the environmental screening factor that suppresses the locally observable Temporal Shear. For an approximately radial binary response,

\begin{equation} \label{eq:vc_squared} v_c^2(s) \simeq \frac{GM}{s} + c^2\,s\,\hat{\mathbf{s}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{\rm obs} \end{equation}

The observed velocity enhancement is therefore the kinematic projection of recovered Temporal Shear, while $\alpha_{\rm sat}$ parameterizes the wide-binary channel response rather than the microscopic coupling $\beta_A$.

2.1 Consistency with Solar-System PPN Constraints

The Cassini Shapiro-delay bound $|\gamma_{\rm PPN} - 1| < 2.3 \times 10^{-5}$ (Bertotti, Iess & Tortora 2003) constrains the source-charge sector $S_\Sigma$ in the solar-vicinity environment, not the wide-binary environment probed here. Two features of the Cassini measurement make the bound weaker than a naive reading suggests for TEP. First, the measurement is a round-trip radio ranging experiment performed during solar conjunction: the signal path samples the Sun's deep potential well, which in TEP corresponds to the screened regime where $S_\Sigma$ is suppressed. The bound $S_\Sigma^{(\odot)} \lesssim 5.8 \times 10^{-6}$ applies to the solar-vicinity environment along the signal path, not to the dilute interstellar medium where the Temporal Shear recovers and wide-binary dynamics originate. Second, the round-trip geometry is reciprocity-even: the conformal clock variation enters as a gradient integral $\int \nabla(\ln A)\cdot d\mathbf{l}$, and the outbound and inbound contributions have opposite signs because the path is traversed in opposite directions, so the clock-sector contribution cancels exactly in the round-trip observable (Smawfield 2025a, Smawfield 2025i). The wide-binary signal, by contrast, is a kinematic observable in the dilute interstellar medium where $S_\Sigma$ has recovered; it probes a different environment and a different measurement geometry from the Cassini bound. The ten-order-of-magnitude gap between the Cassini and wide-binary Yukawa mass requirements is therefore environmental, not sectoral: $S_\Sigma$ is suppressed in the high-density solar vicinity and recovers in the low-density interstellar medium. The density-dependent minimum of the TEP field equation $\nabla^2\phi = V_{,\phi} + \rho_* A_{,\phi}$ provides the environmental transition; this is a general property of the bidirectional conformal field equation, not specific to any particular microscopic completion.

The reconciliation has a precise spatial form. The corpus's master-action screening closure derives a pairwise response of the form $\mathcal R(s) = \mathcal S_\Sigma(X_{\rm env})^2\,y(s)$ (Smawfield 2025a, §7): the mutual shear profile $y(s)$—the exact flux-conserving solution $y\bigl[1+y^2(r_*/r)^4\bigr]=1$ of the stated kinetic completion $P=X-V+X|X|/\Lambda^4$, with $\phi'\propto r^{-2/3}$ deep inside the shell—propagated with the ambient inverse-stiffness factor $\mathcal S_\Sigma(X_{\rm env})=[1+2X_{\rm env}/\Lambda^4]^{-1}$ at the emission and response vertices. The ambient $X_{\rm env}$ is the field of the hierarchy level above the pair, excluding the pair's own co-generated mutual field. Two regimes of the same operator follow. For a hierarchical pair—a test member embedded in a dominant member's pre-existing nonlinear shell—the dominant member's field is the embedding ambient, and the identity $2X_{\rm dom}/\Lambda^4=(1-y)/y$ makes the vertex factors equal to $y(s)$ itself: the response recovers $\mathcal R\sim y^3\sim s^{4}$, the quartic deep-interior asymptote. For a comparable-mass wide binary the mutual field is the measured response rather than a fixed embedding—the two members' field gradients oppose and cancel at the pair's bridge region—so the vertex ambient saturates at the Galactic field and the transition profile is the flux-conserving $s^{4/3}$ law, the exponent the data select (Section 4; effective exponent $\simeq1.3$ through the full forward model). This is a continuous Temporal-Topology recovery rather than a thin-shell boundary—consistent with the rejection of the thin-shell morphology reported in Section 4. The shell radius is the derived law $R_{\rm shell}(M) \equiv r_*(M) = \sqrt{GM/g_t}$, with $g_t = cH_0/(2\beta_A^{2}) \simeq 3.4\times10^{-10}$ m s$^{-2}$, not a per-channel fitted scale. On this single map every existing Solar-System constraint lies deep inside the solar shell, where the hierarchical response $\mathcal R\sim s^4$ applies: $\mathcal R\simeq10^{-23}$ at the Cassini conjunction separation ($1.6\,R_\odot$), $\simeq3\times10^{-15}$ at 1 AU, and $\simeq3\times10^{-11}$ at Saturn's orbit ($9.5$ AU)—the corresponding anomalous acceleration $\delta a \simeq 3.5\times10^{-15}$ m s$^{-2}$ sitting more than two orders of magnitude inside the Cassini-era ephemeris bound—while the Earth–Moon pair lies at $\simeq2\times10^{-15}$ inside the nested terrestrial and solar shells. The pairwise response at the conjunction separation thus sits seventeen orders of magnitude below the already stringent single-body bound $S_\Sigma^{(\odot)}\lesssim5.8\times10^{-6}$, whereas a wide-binary companion at $s\sim R_s$ samples the shell boundary itself. The fitted $R_s = 2{,}646$ AU is therefore a measurement of the solar-type screening-shell radius, not a new screening variable. The corpus's zero-parameter derived value, $R_{\rm shell}\simeq4.7$ kAU at the sample mean mass, sits a factor $\simeq1.8$ above the fitted scale—displaced inward from the asymptotic value by the Galactic ambient floor, in the direction the operator itself predicts (Smawfield 2025a, §7).

The shell reading also fixes the amplitude bookkeeping. With $\beta_A=-1$, a fully unscreened source would contribute $2\beta_A^{2}g_N = 2g_N$ of extra centripetal acceleration—a plateau at $\alpha_{\rm sat}=\sqrt{3}-1\simeq0.73$. The observed plateau corresponds to $+0.87\,g_N$, approximately 43% of the unscreened response: a partial recovery bounded by the Galactic ambient floor rather than a return to unsuppressed charge, with an implied per-body factor $S_{\rm body}\simeq0.66$ comparable to the corpus's derived ambient suppression at the solar circle ($\mathcal S_\Sigma\simeq0.74$; Smawfield 2025a, §7). The same shell map closes the corpus's remaining constraints on this class of source. At Oort-cloud separations $s\sim10^{4}$–$10^{5}$ AU the pairwise factor is $\gtrsim0.95$: bodies there orbit in the recovered-shear regime, the domain analyzed by the companion outer-Solar-System boundary study (Smawfield 2026, Paper 36). Terrestrial clock networks, at 1 AU, sit deep inside the solar shell (solar contribution $\mathcal S_{\rm eff}\sim10^{-15}$) and measure the nested terrestrial well's own shear, consistent with the GNSS correlation phenomenology (Smawfield 2025b, 2025c). And the ambient-floor-limited response surviving beyond every stellar shell supplies the effective-gravity contribution invoked by the rotation-curve sector (Smawfield 2025g). The screening shell is therefore not an additional assumption but the spatial semantics of the single operator $\mathcal S_\Sigma(\mathcal E)$ evaluated across separations: one map, bounded inside by Cassini, measured at its boundary by wide binaries, and read off asymptotically by the Galactic field.

2.2 The Screening Radius

$R_T$ here denotes the projected separation at which the Temporal Shear suppression transitions from the screened to active-shear regime, related to but distinct from the GNSS correlation length $\lambda_T$ (which characterises the radial relaxation scale within a gravitational well) and the geometric saturation radius $R_T(M, \rho_T, \epsilon_{\rm env})$ derived in Paper 6 (which characterises the system-level boundary condition for a self-gravitating source of mass $M$). The wide-binary screening radius $R_s$ introduced below in Equation (\ref{eq:screening_radius}) is the operative benchmark scale for this transition.

For a binary of total mass $M$, the locally active Temporal Shear $\Sigma_\mu \equiv \nabla_\mu \ln A(\phi)$ is suppressed continuously by the environmental and source state rather than gated by an on/off density threshold (Smawfield 2025a, Section 7). As the binary's internal Newtonian potential becomes shallow enough that the source-charge sector no longer fully suppresses the locally observable Temporal Shear, the residual gradient of $\ln A$ generates an additional kinematic enhancement above the Keplerian baseline. The transition is therefore characterized by a continuous recovery of the Temporal Shear, parameterized below by an effective screening radius $R_s$.

Within the Galaxy, however, the binary is not isolated. The TEP framework itself does not commit to a single microscopic screening mechanism: Vainshtein, Galileon, DBI, and symmetron mechanisms are treated as candidate completions of the conformal-sector screening (Smawfield 2025a, §A4, §7), with the defining ontology being the continuous suppression of the locally observable Temporal Shear $\Sigma_\mu \equiv \nabla_\mu \ln A(\phi)$ by source-charge sector, environmental state, and boundary conditions; the inverse-power chameleon branch is excluded under the TEP coupling convention, since $\beta_A = -1$ with $\phi > 0$ in potential wells admits no admissible minimum for $V \propto \phi^{-n}$ (Smawfield 2025a, constraint F1). The quantitative benchmark for the wide-binary transition scale is therefore an empirical environmental-density parameterization rather than a specific potential. Any admissible completion supplies a density-dependent screened equilibrium—a strong effective scalar response in dense environments that flattens the Temporal Topology and drives $\Sigma_\mu \to 0$ continuously rather than via thin-shell matching. The surrounding halo and disk already drive $\phi$ toward this screened configuration, so the transition scale relevant to a wide binary is set not by $\rho_T$ directly but by the residual local screening floor that the binary samples. This floor admits a closed-form parameterization that serves as a benchmark; admissible completions would yield qualitatively similar but quantitatively different forms.

\begin{equation} \label{eq:rho_floor} \rho_{\rm floor} = \epsilon_{\rm env}\,\rho_T \end{equation}
\begin{equation} \label{eq:screening_radius} R_s = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{g_t}} \propto M^{1/2}, \qquad \rho_{\rm eff}(R_s) \equiv \frac{3M}{4\pi R_s^3} = \rho_{\rm floor} = \epsilon_{\rm env}\,\rho_T \end{equation}

Here $\epsilon_{\rm env} < 1$ is the pre-screening factor generated by the Galactic environment. The left-hand member is the canonical derived radius law of the nested operator (the flux-conserving shell radius; Section 2.1); the right-hand member is the equivalent enclosed-density diagnostic coordinate of the same transition—for the canonical law $\rho_{\rm eff}$ scales as $M^{-1/2}$ at fixed environment rather than as a fixed density—and it serves as a calibration benchmark rather than a unique prediction of the underlying theory. Two of the three ingredients—$\rho_T$ and the field equation whose solution determines $\epsilon_{\rm env}$—are already derived in earlier work. The Temporal Topology saturation scale $\rho_T \approx 20$ g/cm$^3$ is anchored by GNSS atomic-clock networks and supported by SPARC rotation-curve slopes and compact-object consistency tests (Smawfield 2025g). The density-dependent effective potential $V_{\rm eff}(\phi;\rho)$ whose ground state sets the local screening floor follows from the TEP two-metric action together with the scalar self-interaction sector (Smawfield 2025a, §7). The character of the transition is further tested by comparing the canonical phenomenological TEP recovery profile to alternative transition morphologies. As shown in Section 4, both the thin-shell exterior profile itself and a sigmoid step-like proxy are rejected relative to the TEP exponential ($\Delta\chi^2 = +613.0$ and $+131.5$, respectively; Table 4.1). This rejection is naturally explained by the continuous Temporal Topology framework: the field gradient recovers smoothly, not via a step-function onset.

The canonical cross-scale anchor is now the master-action closure itself: the derived acceleration scale $g_t = cH_0/(2\beta_A^2) \simeq 3.4\times10^{-10}$ m/s$^2$ yields the radius law $R_s = \sqrt{GM/g_t} \simeq 4.7$ kAU at the sample mean mass, a factor $\simeq1.8$ above the fitted $R_s$ (Section 2.2; Smawfield 2025a, §7)—the physically expected direction, since the ambient Galactic field raises the environmental floor and displaces the transition inward. An older SPARC-extracted benchmark, $g_{\rm TEP} \approx 5\times10^{-10}$ m/s$^2$ (Smawfield 2025g), returns $3{,}831$ AU (factor 1.5), and a further external-field divisor $\eta=2$ tightens that empirical normalization to $2{,}709$ AU (3\%)—but that tightening is a tuned factor rather than a derived one, and both empirical anchors are superseded as predictions by the canonical $g_t$. The wide-binary $R_s$ is therefore not an unanchored binary-sector scale: its order of magnitude is fixed by the cross-scale architecture, and the residual factor $\simeq1.8$ is the open environmental-coefficient question recorded by the corpus.

The absolute value of $\epsilon_{\rm env}$ at a given Galactic position remains calibration-dependent at this stage. A semantic point should be stated plainly: the quantity $\rho_{\rm eff} = 3M/(4\pi R_s^3) \approx 9.2 \times 10^{-18}$ g/cm$^3$ evaluated at $M \approx 1.2\,M_\odot$ is the binary's own enclosed mean density at the transition separation—some six orders of magnitude above the ambient ISM density—not an environmental density floor. Under the canonical operator the transition criterion is the ratio of the pair's internal field gradient to the ambient environmental shear floor; $\rho_{\rm eff}$ is an equivalent diagnostic coordinate of that criterion (for $R_s = \sqrt{GM/g_t}$ it scales as $M^{-1/2}$, not as a fixed density), while $\epsilon_{\rm env} \approx 4.6 \times 10^{-19}$ is the numerical value of the benchmark parameterization $\rho_{\rm floor} = \epsilon_{\rm env}\rho_T$ rather than a physical density. The height dependence of $\epsilon_{\rm env}$ is then semi-predictive within that parameterization: the screening radius is taken to scale as a power law in ambient baryonic density, $R_s \propto \rho_{\rm amb}^{p}$, so the ratio of screening radii at two Galactic heights depends only on the ambient density ratio and the scaling exponent $p$. As shown in Section 5.2, the benchmark value $p = 1/2$ combined with a standard three-component Galactic density model reproduces the observed environmental $R_s$ ratio within $\sim 1\sigma$. The TEP-native statement is qualitative—more strongly screening environmental states more strongly suppress the locally observable Temporal Shear—while the quantitative exponent $p$ is an empirical property of the environmental response, not of a specified microscopic completion. (The inverse-power chameleon form that historically motivates writing $p = 1/(n+1)$ is excluded under the TEP coupling convention; Smawfield 2025a, F1.) Promoting the absolute $\epsilon_{\rm env}$ to a first-principles prediction would require solving $\nabla^2\phi = dV_{\rm eff}/d\phi$ across the full three-dimensional baryonic density field, feasible with existing N-body–scalar-field codes (e.g., Llinares, Mota & Winther 2014). The large gap between $\rho_T$ and $\rho_{\rm eff}$ does not imply two unrelated saturation scales; it reflects the many orders of magnitude of pre-screening already absorbed by the Galactic halo and disk before the binary's own potential is tested. The radius $R_s$ is thus the screening-shell boundary introduced in Section 2.1: the surface on which the pair's enclosed mean density falls to the effective environmental floor, equivalently parameterized by the corpus's derived transition law $R_{\rm shell}(M) = \sqrt{GM/g_t}$. The density and acceleration parameterizations of the shell boundary identify the same physical transition, and their distinct mass scalings are discriminated directly by the mass-ordered transition itself (Section 4), whose measured exponent is consistent with the derived $\sqrt{GM/g_t}$ law.

2.3 The Velocity Enhancement Profile

Within this framework, the kinematic profile is expected to satisfy three qualitative constraints: (i) $\tilde{v} \to 1$ in the fully screened limit $s \ll R_s$; (ii) a monotonic transition near $s \sim R_s$; and (iii) saturation at a bounded enhancement $\tilde{v} \to 1 + \alpha_{\rm sat}$ for $s \gg R_s$. These follow directly from conformal-sector screening and do not depend on the detailed nonlinear field dynamics.

These qualitative features are not merely asserted; they follow from the continuous Temporal Topology of the conformal scalar field. In the TEP framework (Smawfield 2025a, §7), screening operates not via the thin-shell approximation but through the continuous spatial profile of $\ln A(\phi)$ (the Temporal Topology), governed by the non-linear superposition of conformal-factor gradients (the Temporal Shear, $\Sigma_\mu \equiv \nabla_\mu \ln A = \alpha(\phi)\,\nabla_\mu\phi$). In dense environments the source-charge sector and environmental state drive the locally observable shear toward zero, flattening the Temporal Topology. As that suppression weakens with the diluting ambient environment—rather than crossing a binary on/off density threshold—the Temporal Shear recovers continuously, producing a smooth transition from the screened to the active-shear regime. This Temporal-Topology framing is independent of the specific microscopic screening completion; the empirical density-scaling benchmark used in §2.2 is consistent with the same continuous-suppression ontology shared by the admissible completions (Vainshtein, Galileon, DBI, symmetron).

The classical thin-shell formalism (Khoury & Weltman 2004; Burrage & Sakstein 2018) provides useful context. For a screened mass $M$ with screening radius $R_s$ embedded in a background of effective scalar mass $m_{\rm bg} = \sqrt{V_{\rm eff}''(\phi_{\rm bg})}$, the exterior scalar field profile obtained under thin-shell matching conditions is:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:field_profile} \phi(r) = \phi_{\rm bg} - C\,\frac{R_s}{r}\,e^{-m_{\rm bg}(r - R_s)}, \qquad r > R_s \end{equation}

where $C$ is determined by the thin-shell matching and the $R_s/r$ prefactor arises from the thin-shell boundary. The corresponding unscreening fraction $f(s) = 1 - (R_s/s)\,e^{-m_{\rm bg}(s - R_s)}$ produces onset–rise–saturation morphology with a step-like character at $R_s$.

However, the TEP Temporal Topology framework replaces the thin-shell approximation with a continuous field profile. Without a step-function matching condition, the $R_s/s$ geometric prefactor—which encodes the thin-shell boundary—does not arise. Continuous Temporal Topology therefore requires a smooth, one-scale recovery from the screened state toward a finite asymptotic Temporal Shear. The canonical wide-binary realization of this relaxation is the pure exponential:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:velocity_profile} \tilde{v}(s) = 1 + \alpha_{\rm sat}\bigl(1 - e^{-s/R_s}\bigr) \end{equation}

where $R_s$ is the characteristic scale of the Temporal Topology transition and $\alpha_{\rm sat}$ is the saturation amplitude set by the asymptotic Temporal Shear. This is adopted as the canonical fitting function throughout the analysis. Unlike the thin-shell formula, the pure exponential is the canonical one-scale realization of continuous Temporal Topology in the wide-binary sector, containing exactly the two physical quantities required by the TEP screening geometry: the recovery scale $R_s$ and the asymptotic response $\alpha_{\rm sat}$. Equation (\ref{eq:velocity_profile}) is the minimal smooth realization of the TEP onset–recovery–saturation morphology; the transition scale and environmental ordering, rather than the exact exponential shape alone, carry the physical inference. Mass and environmental variation broaden this canonical profile rather than changing its underlying onset–recovery–saturation structure, as demonstrated explicitly by the mass-convolved TEP model in Section 4.1. A literal first-principles prediction for the precise profile shape would require solving the full coupled system $\nabla^2\phi = dV_{\rm eff}/d\phi$ in the realistic potential of each binary within its Galactic environment. What the data can test at this stage is whether the morphological class of profile predicted by continuous Temporal Topology—finite onset, smooth exponential rise, bounded saturation—is preferred over the alternatives (scale-free MOND, constant boost, flat Newtonian, or the sharper step-like transition of the thin-shell sigmoid). The sensitivity of the inferred transition scale to the choice of functional form is assessed explicitly in Section 4 by fitting sigmoid and double-exponential alternatives, and the resulting spread is absorbed into the systematic uncertainty budget. As shown there, all three transition models agree on a $\sim 2{,}000$–$3{,}200$ AU onset scale, confirming that the inferred $R_s$ is a robust feature of the data rather than an artifact of any particular parametric choice. Notably, the data reject both the stated thin-shell exterior profile ($\Delta\chi^2 = +613.0$ versus the TEP exponential) and the sigmoid step-like proxy ($\Delta\chi^2 = +131.5$). This rejection is naturally explained by the continuous Temporal Topology framework: the field gradient recovers smoothly, not via a step-function onset.

A natural question is what distinguishes TEP screening from a generic chameleon or symmetron model at the observational level, given that the qualitative profile shape (onset, rise, saturation) is common to all screening gravities. The answer lies in three features that go beyond morphology. First, TEP makes a quantitative cross-scale prediction: the same universal conformal sector and Temporal Topology saturation architecture that govern atomic and compact-object phenomenology (Smawfield 2025g) also set the Galactic screening floor. The wide-binary transition scale is therefore not theoretically isolated within the binary sector, but is anchored to independently measured TEP scales. Second, the environmental test (Section 5) probes the environmental dependence of the locally observable Temporal Shear generated by the conformal sector; in the density-scaling benchmark this dependence projects primarily onto ambient baryonic density. Third, continuous Temporal Topology predicts a smoother recovery than the thin-shell approximation of standard chameleon models; in the canonical wide-binary realization adopted here, that recovery is represented by the pure exponential $\tilde{v}(s) = 1 + \alpha_{\rm sat}(1 - e^{-s/R_s})$ rather than the thin-shell form $f(s) = 1 - (R_s/s)\,e^{-m_{\rm bg}(s-R_s)}$ with its geometric $R_s/s$ prefactor. Notably, the data already favor this prediction: the stated thin-shell profile $f(s) = 1 - (R_s/s)\,e^{-m_{\rm bg}(s-R_s)}$ is rejected at $\Delta\chi^2 = +613.0$ and the sigmoid step-like proxy at $\Delta\chi^2 = +131.5$, both relative to the TEP exponential (Table 4.1). Future observations could sharpen these distinctions: Gaia DR4 radial velocities would enable three-dimensional deprojection, testing whether the proper-time signature of TEP—in which the same Temporal Shear that produces the kinematic enhancement also rescales matter-frame clock rates as $\mathrm{d}\tau/\mathrm{d}t = A(\phi)$—produces a different velocity-anisotropy pattern than a generic screened scalar model that does not make the TEP-specific identification of the conformal gradient with dynamical proper-time geometry. Measurements of the transition radius as a function of Galactocentric radius $R_{\rm gc}$ would further discriminate among candidate completions, since the density-scaling benchmark adopted here predicts a screening floor set by the local baryonic density rather than the total gravitational potential (Burrage & Sakstein 2018), whereas symmetron- or Vainshtein-style completions would imply different gradient-profile dependences—a pattern that maps directly onto the underlying Temporal Shear morphology rather than onto a uniform acceleration threshold.
